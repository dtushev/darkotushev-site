June 24, 2025

I’ve sat through enough AI strategy meetings to know the usual arc: someone pulls up a slide with a labyrinth of data pipelines, someone else uses the word “orchestration” three times in a single sentence, and suddenly it feels like we’re launching a spaceship instead of serving people. Don’t get me wrong - models, infrastructure, and systems matter. But they’re not what will determine whether your organization thrives in this new era. The real differentiator? It’s not just your tech. It’s your touch. It’s empathy. That quiet, often-overlooked force that turns good ideas into meaningful change.

Not as a buzzword. Not as a soft skill buried on slide 47 of the org culture deck. Empathy as your operating principle. Your differentiator. Your strategic edge.

The more I work at the intersection of retail, fashion, and AI, the more I realize: ***if your technology doesn’t make people’s lives better, easier, more human, more connected, then what exactly is the point?***

We’re living through a generational shift. AI is reshaping industries, workflows, job descriptions, and even our collective imagination. And in that transformation, there’s a temptation to lead with speed. Ship fast. Implement now. Race to keep up.

But real leadership was never about playing catch-up. It’s not about reacting to the latest trend or rushing to check a box marked “AI-enabled.” Leadership is about having the clarity to see beyond the noise and the courage to decide how you want to show up in that future. It’s about setting the tone, not just meeting the tempo. ***The best leaders don’t just adapt to what’s coming - they shape it, with intention, with vision, and above all, with humanity.***

Empathy isn’t the thing that slows us down. It’s the thing that keeps us from running in the wrong direction.

Too often, I hear AI talked about in extremes. On one side: utopian hype about infinite productivity. On the other hand, dystopian dread that we’re automating ourselves out of existence. The reality, as it usually is, lives in the messy middle. AI has enormous potential - but only if we shape it with care.

John Dewey once said, “A problem well put is half solved.” The way we frame AI’s role in our organizations will define what we build and who it benefits. ***So we have to start with people. The ones using the tools. The ones impacted by the decisions. The ones whose trust we need to earn and keep.***

Design thinking got this right long before AI showed up at the party. Empathize. Define. Ideate. Prototype. Test. Yet somewhere between data ingestion and model optimization, we forgot step one. We forgot to ask what matters to the people we claim to serve.

I’ve seen firsthand how empathy changes the outcome of an AI project. A pilot that works in theory but frustrates store associates in practice? That’s not innovation. That’s disruption without discernment. But when we pause, listen, and adapt, when we respond to the lived experience of our users, we build solutions that stick.

And here’s the business case, because yes, there is one. ***Products grounded in empathy drive better adoption.*** ***Experiences rooted in real human needs create loyalty.*** AI that understands context reduces friction, returns, and complaints. ***Empathy, it turns out, is measurable. You’ll find it in your KPIs if you know where to look.***

Strategically, empathy helps you see around corners. It reveals not just what’s happening, but ***why.*** It surfaces unmet needs, quiet frustrations, and latent potential. And that intelligence? That’s what the best leaders use to make bold, enduring decisions.

***Empathy also builds trust, and trust is the currency of transformation.*** If your teams don’t trust your intent, they’ll resist your roadmap. If your customers don’t trust your experience, they’ll leave your brand. AI will either reinforce that trust or quietly erode it. The choice is ours.

I know it might feel counterintuitive. We’re trained to prioritize logic, efficiency, and output. But as Brené Brown put it, “Clear is kind.***” And being clear about the human impact of our systems is the kindest and most strategic thing we can do.***

This isn’t about being nice. It’s about being real. About telling the truth, especially when it’s uncomfortable. About acknowledging that ***automation without accountability can widen gaps, not close them.*** The decisions we make now will shape work, creativity, and identity for years to come.

Empathy requires courage. It requires slowing down enough to ask better questions. It requires widening the room to include voices that don’t always sit at the tech table. And it requires remembering that ***leadership isn’t just about what we build, it’s about how we build it, and for whom.***

So as we navigate this moment, this inflection point where technology meets humanity, I believe our mandate is clear.

## **Lead with empathy. Embed it into your strategy, your processes, and your metrics. Make it the first step in every project, and the last thing you check before you ship. Treat it not as a moral accessory, but as a core competency.**

Because in the age of AI, the companies that win won’t be the ones that move the fastest. ***They’ll be the ones who remember what it means to be human.***

And they’ll lead the way, not with code, but with care.
