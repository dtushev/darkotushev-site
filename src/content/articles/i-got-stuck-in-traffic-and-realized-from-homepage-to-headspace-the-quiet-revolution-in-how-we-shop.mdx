Last weekend, I took my family to New York City. It was supposed to be a simple, relaxing getaway. You know, the kind where you leave early, beat traffic, and maybe feel smug about it.

Instead, we spent four hours inching down the highway because, of course, someone thought peak Saturday was the perfect time to repave an entire lane with one lonely guy and a traffic cone army. Bravo.

What should’ve been a two-hour drive turned into an existential podcast, three snack negotiations, and a lot of wondering how we’ve got AI that can write sonnets, but no one’s figured out New York traffic. Honestly… everything evolves. Phones. Shopping. Mars rovers.

But roadwork scheduling in NYC? Untouched since the Bronze Age.

In the backseat, my brother-in-law was snoring with an Olympic-level commitment. My wife was quietly scrolling through old photos and listening to music. And I, stuck in traffic, hands free for once, did something I didn’t plan to do. I opened up ChatGPT and I planned our entire upcoming trip to Macedonia. Then I ordered family gifts… just by asking Alexa. No apps. No tabs. No scrolling. Just words. And it felt easy. Frighteningly easy. By default, stuck in traffic, I thought - “Alright, might as well put my hands to use.” But then it hit me... I didn’t need my hands at all. I could just talk my way through it - and get things done, like magic. That moment stuck with me. Because for the first time, I realized something that had been creeping up for a while:

**More and more, shopping isn’t starting on a homepage. Not always. Not by default.**

It’s starting in a conversation. A quick ask. A moment of intent that happens off-screen. And in the very near future, faster than most of us realize, that conversation won’t even start with me, with us. It’ll start with an AI. Our digital twin. Our super-efficient friend who knows we're traveling soon, remembers Ella’s birthday is next week, and has already filtered for waterproof, under $100, arrive by Tuesday gifts... before we had our first sip of coffee.

That’s not science fiction. That’s just where we’re headed. Sounds wild, I know. But it’s happening.

That’s exciting. And also... kind of terrifying. Because if that’s what a car ride in 2025 feels like, what’s the world going to feel like in 2030? Or 2035? **We’re in a moment of real change.**

Not the slow, buzzwordy kind. The real kind, the kind that sneaks up on you and then one day just… happens. And when you work in tech, retail, product, or anything that touches customers, you start to notice: Times are changing, and even though the screen is still here. It’s not calling all the shots anymore.

For years, we treated the screen like the North Star. We designed around it. We optimized it. We made sure every button, banner, PDP, and scroll felt “just right.” We built for the screen.

Now? **We should start thinking about building through it.** Let me explain.

For the majority of eccomerce existence, people typed in a URL, landed on a homepage, clicked around, and made a decision. It was predictable. Linear. Measurable. Funnel-shaped.

Today, we are at the inception of a new concept that will shape commerce for years to come. It's becoming normalized to hear our loved ones saying things like:

“Hey, can you find me a backpack that’s carry-on size, waterproof, and under $100?” It's for Ella's birthday - need to be here before Wednesday." And before you have the chance to respond, Alexa, or perplexity, or whoever, will say, " Sure, I got you. "

That’s it. **One sentence = one intent = one action.**

The interface is still a screen, well, sometimes. But really, it can be anything now. Any device that’s connected, that listens, that talks back. The main point? Screens aren’t the start of the experience anymore. They’re just one possible stop along the way, or not there at all.

What matters it’s the surface beneath it. The real action? It’s happening in the conversation - whether it’s voice, chat, gesture, or prompt. **We’ve entered the era of agentic interfaces.** Where we don’t just browse. We brief. Where we don’t just click. We command. And when that’s the default, what happens to the beautiful funnel we spent years obsessing over?

**This isn’t a fresh coat of paint, it’s like the whole house got up, moved neighborhoods, and forgot to leave a forwarding address. Because it’s not just the "screen part" of the journey that’s changing. It’s all of it.**

From how we discover products to how we get help after they arrive, every phase of the shopping lifecycle is quietly transforming. Not with fireworks, but with small, meaningful shifts.

**Discovery** used to start with a homepage. Now it might start with a whisper to your new AI Glasses. Or a typed question in your chat app. “I'm sad. What should we do?” - The bot might say - "Let's go Shopping - that always make you smile. Do you want to see the newest collection? " Boom. Five curated options appear, pulled from a dozen storefronts you never visited on your glasses, or phone, or it just describes them for you in detail.

**Evaluation** used to mean comparing tabs, reading reviews, and sorting filters. In our example, it’s a conversation. Your agent knows what you like. What you’ve returned. What your friend just bought. It narrows your choices before you even ask. It filters on your behalf. It suggests based on your mood.

**Ordering** used to be the big finale. Now it’s invisible. A sentence, a nod, a confirmation ping, or completely independent of you, as you told it 4 months ago to find good deals for your 20 year anniversary, and it just happened to have a flash sale. It’s not a journey. It’s a moment. But behind that moment? A hundred micro-decisions your system has to make flawlessly.

**Fulfillment** is under pressure, too. Because a smart assistant doesn’t care about your internal APIs. It cares that the product gets there on time. That it fits. That it works. It will notify you when your inventory is stale. It will remember if the box was beaten up last time.

And **post-purchase?** That might be the most human moment of all. Because the agent helped them shop, but now something has gone wrong. It didn’t arrive. It doesn’t fit. It disappointed. And suddenly, they’re not talking to the agent anymore. **They’re talking to you.**

That’s the moment that matters. That’s the moment they remember. That’s the moment where brands are made - or lost. **So yes, the screen is still here. But it’s no longer the main character.** It’s the quiet helper, the conduit. The journey? It’s happening in real life again. And we have to be ready to meet it. **This also means that your brand’s API becomes your voice. Your product schema becomes your storefront. Your trustworthiness becomes your new conversion rate.**

Take a breath. (I did too.)

Because yeah, this new world? It’s wild. And it’s not just about “letting go of control.” It’s about showing up differently. Here’s the thing. If we want our brands to exist in this next chapter of shopping, we’ve got to start thinking like the agents. **That means being findable, understandable, trustworthy… and honestly, kinda helpful. If the agent doesn’t trust what we’re putting out there, we don’t get surfaced. It’s that simple.**

And no, I’m not talking about some mystical trust fall with a robot. I mean stuff like: **Is your data clean?** Like, “this-is-a-medium-not-a-weird-number-code” clean. **Can your products be explained easily?** "Why this one? Oh, because it has great reviews, ships in two days, and doesn’t fall apart in the wash.”) **Do you show up in the right places?** Not just your site. Think Reddit, reviews, blogs, all the places the models are learning from. **Can an AI agent actually use your stuff?** Meaning: Do you have real APIs, or are you hiding behind PDF catalogs from 2009?

This is where **Generative Engine Optimization** sneaks in.

Not just SEO. GEO. It's how you show up in a prompt, not just a Google search. You’re not ranking anymore. You’re being recommended. That changes the whole game. You have to be clear. Real. Fast. You have to be something the agent can trust enough to say: “Yep. This is the one. This will solve their problem.” And if you’re not?

**You’re invisible.**

But if you are? You become the answer to someone’s “I-need-this-now” moment. Their “what-do-I-buy-for-Dad” panic. Their “last-minute-pack-for-my-kid’s-camp” scramble.

**That’s the win. That’s the goal. Not perfect UX. Not a shinier banner. Just... being ready. To be picked. By an agent. For a person. And that starts now. But remember - the agent might place the order. But the person still opens the box. And they’ll remember if it made them smile… or made them sigh.**

Honestly? I don’t have a five-step plan to make this easy. We’re all learning this together. We're in a moment that feels like early mobile, or even earlier internet. Except this time, there’s no slow ramp-up. No time to get comfy. Just this:

**Try, test, listen. Adjust. Repeat.** Be brave enough to start small. Be wise enough to bet on the right horse, or dog, or team, whatever works for you to get this part. And be kind enough to remember the people on the other side of the prompt.

Five years ago, most of us would’ve laughed at the idea of a personal AI assistant in your pocket for $20/month. Now? We’re planning family trips and buying gifts from the backseat of a car, using nothing but our voice. So yeah, this future is coming faster than we think. Let’s get ready. Let’s get curious. And let’s not forget to bring our empathy with us.

**Because even if the screen fades into the background… the human never should.**
